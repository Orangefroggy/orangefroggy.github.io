Before you start

This page takes whatever photo you upload, scales it down, and slowly swaps its pixels around, keeping any swap that makes the image look a little more like the cat above. It starts from your actual photo, not scrambled noise, so it plays out like a smooth morph from your photo into the target. Nothing new is ever painted in, it's just your own photo's colors finding new spots.

  1. Upload a photo, or just try it with random noise first.
  2. Pick a resolution. Small is fast and forgiving. Big looks sharper if it works, but takes longer and can turn out noisy.
  3. Hit "Begin evolving" and watch it go. It watches itself for the sharpest-looking point and stops there on its own.
  4. Download the result once you like what you see.

Since a photo's colors are fixed, don't expect a perfect copy of the cat, especially from a photo with a totally different palette. That's part of the fun.

What's actually happening: your photo gets scaled down to the chosen resolution, and evolution starts from that photo exactly as it looks, no shuffling into noise first. From there, the algorithm tries thousands of random swap(i, j) moves per generation, and only keeps a swap if it makes the image look a bit more like the target. Nothing gets painted or invented, the same colors just keep changing addresses, which is why it reads as a smooth morph from your photo into the target rather than a jump to static. It stops itself once it hits 99.95% match to the target, or earlier if it genuinely runs out of improving swaps to make, rather than running for a fixed amount of time.
